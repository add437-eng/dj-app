Springboard

Start with one song and get a DJ set that flows by key, tempo and style, with a plain-English reason for every transition. Built as a companion to Spotify's Mix feature, which shows each track's BPM and Camelot key but leaves the curating to you.

Tap a key on the wheel to see which keys mix well with it.

Why I built this

As a casual DJ, I've found Spotify's Mix feature to be a great springboard. It shows every track's BPM and Camelot key, and Smart Reorder sorts a playlist by them. I wanted to take it a step further and understand which songs belong together, what to add next, and how to get better at mixing.

Springboard is my prototype of that next step for aspiring DJs. Pick a starting song, and it builds a set that follows the Camelot wheel, explains every transition in plain English, and grows as you add your own tracks. It's also my way of exploring how Spotify's features could help me move from curation to creation.

Start here. The set below opens on my Le Freak set. Look at the key path, read a few transition notes, and then try another starting song or drag tracks around in My crate.

Every track you own, organized around the current starting song. It shows what's in the set, what could fit, and what doesn't and why. Drag tracks by their handle to set your own play order. How mixing works

The conventions the engine follows, so you learn the why, not just the order.

Camelot keys

Every song has a musical key. The Camelot system turns the 24 keys into a clock, numbered 1–12, with A for minor keys (moodier) and B for major keys (brighter).

Keys that sit next to each other share most of their notes, so they blend without clashing.

Safe moves

Same code (8A to 8A) is the smoothest blend. One step (8A to 9A or 7A) works too, where +1 lifts the mood and −1 relaxes it. Swapping the letter (8A to 8B) keeps the same notes and moves from minor to major or back.

Energy boosts (+2, or +7 for a semitone up) work with quick cuts, not long blends.

Tempo

DJs usually nudge a track's speed by up to about 6% before it sounds unnatural. 118 into 124 is fine; 100 into 125 is not.

Half and double time also work, so a 64 BPM track can feel like 128.

Genre tempo ranges

Hip-hop 85–100. Funk and soul 95–115. Disco and nu-disco 110–125. House 118–130. Techno 125–140. Drum and bass 170–175.

Energy and phrasing

A good set tells a story. It warms up, builds, peaks and releases. Change one thing at a time, so a big key change goes with a steady tempo, not a tempo jump too.

Start blends at the top of a phrase, usually every 16 or 32 beats, where the song naturally changes section.
